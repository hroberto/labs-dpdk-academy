   12       7.30       137.0                88 ns           10.1x

   32       3.17       315.6               101 ns           23.2x
   64       2.47       404.1               158 ns           29.8x

  The latency of ONE access is ~74 ns and does not change in any row.
  What changes is how many of them happen at the same time.

  With K = 1 this machine does 13.6 M accesses/s -- BELOW the 14.9 M
  packets/s of the 10 GbE line rate with 64 B frames. A single
  dependent access per packet already loses the rate, before any
  processing. With K = 64 there is 2615% of headroom left.

  And the price: the batch of 64 is only ready in 158 ns, against 74 ns
  for the lone access. Throughput bought with latency -- the budget of
  67.2 ns per packet says how much of that trade you can afford.

        12       12.46        80.3         963.5           46%

  From 1 to 12 cores the AGGREGATE throughput grows 5.6x, not 12x.
  Each core, alone, did 173.4 M accesses/s; with 12 active it does
  80.3 M -- 46% of what it did alone. Bandwidth does not multiply
  per core: it is divided.

  And the design consequence: sizing a data plane from the
  measurement of ONE lcore overestimates the whole system. The number
  that matters is the bottom row of this table, not the top one.
