McKenney's Table 3.1, reproduced on this machine
(25 samples per measurement; times in ns)

  Reference: McKenney, "Is Parallel Programming Hard...", Table 3.1,
  measured on an AMD Opteron 844, 1.8 GHz, 4 sockets.
  Here: 1 socket, 2 L3 domains. Measured clock period: 0.182 ns.

BEST CASE - the cache line is already on this core

  measurement                           median  p25-p75 (IQR)     cycles       disp
  ---------------------------------- ---------  --------------- --------      -----
  CAS, best case                          7.18  7.18-7.21             40        0.5%  
  lock, best case                         2.09  2.08-2.10             12        0.7%  

CACHE MISS - the line is on another core and has to migrate

  measurement                           median  p25-p75 (IQR)     cycles       disp
  ---------------------------------- ---------  --------------- --------      -----
  plain miss, same L3 domain             21.25  21.16-21.47          117        1.5%  
  CAS with miss, same L3 domain          20.20  20.13-23.58          111       17.1% !
  plain miss, OTHER L3 domain            82.16  82.14-82.21          452        0.1%  
  CAS with miss, OTHER L3 domain         81.66  81.64-81.70          449        0.1%  

  For comparison, McKenney's values in CYCLES (0.6 ns period):
    CAS best case    63 | lock best case   109
    cache miss      232 | CAS with miss     510

  McKenney's system has 4 sockets; there 'another core' can
  mean another socket. Here the equivalent distinction is between the
  two L3 domains -- the last two rows against the two
  before them. That is the SOSP 2013 conclusion: the scalability of
  synchronisation is, above all, a property of the hardware.
