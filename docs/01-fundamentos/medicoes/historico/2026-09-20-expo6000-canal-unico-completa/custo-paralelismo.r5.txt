   K   ns/access   M accesses/s   batch of K ready in   throughput gain
  ---  ---------   -----------   ---------------------   --------------
    1      76.09        13.1                76 ns            1.0x
    2      37.96        26.3                76 ns            2.0x
    4      20.72        48.3                83 ns            3.7x
    8      10.87        92.0                87 ns            7.0x
   12       7.57       132.0                91 ns           10.0x
   16       5.86       170.6                94 ns           13.0x
   32       3.48       287.2               111 ns           21.9x
   64       2.78       359.5               178 ns           27.4x

  The latency of ONE access is ~76 ns and does not change in any row.
  What changes is how many of them happen at the same time.

  With K = 1 this machine does 13.1 M accesses/s -- BELOW the 14.9 M
  packets/s of the 10 GbE line rate with 64 B frames. A single
  dependent access per packet already loses the rate, before any
  processing. With K = 64 there is 2316% of headroom left.

  And the price: the batch of 64 is only ready in 178 ns, against 76 ns
  for the lone access. Throughput bought with latency -- the budget of
  67.2 ns per packet says how much of that trade you can afford.

         1        6.20       161.2         161.2          100%

         8       14.32        69.8         558.6           43%
        12       21.26        47.0         564.3           29%

  From 1 to 12 cores the AGGREGATE throughput grows 3.5x, not 12x.
  Each core, alone, did 161.2 M accesses/s; with 12 active it does
  47.0 M -- 29% of what it did alone. Bandwidth does not multiply
  per core: it is divided.

  And the design consequence: sizing a data plane from the
  measurement of ONE lcore overestimates the whole system. The number
  that matters is the bottom row of this table, not the top one.
