   K   ns/access   M accesses/s   batch of K ready in   throughput gain
  ---  ---------   -----------   ---------------------   --------------
    1      89.42        11.2                89 ns            1.0x
    2      43.79        22.8                88 ns            2.0x
    4      23.65        42.3                95 ns            3.8x
    8      12.63        79.2               101 ns            7.1x
   12       8.68       115.2               104 ns           10.3x
   16       6.76       147.8               108 ns           13.2x
   32       4.02       248.6               129 ns           22.2x
   64       3.26       306.6               209 ns           27.4x

  The latency of ONE access is ~89 ns and does not change in any row.
  What changes is how many of them happen at the same time.

  With K = 1 this machine does 11.2 M accesses/s -- BELOW the 14.9 M
  packets/s of the 10 GbE line rate with 64 B frames. A single
  dependent access per packet already loses the rate, before any
  processing. With K = 64 there is 1961% of headroom left.

  And the price: the batch of 64 is only ready in 209 ns, against 89 ns
  for the lone access. Throughput bought with latency -- the budget of
  67.2 ns per packet says how much of that trade you can afford.

        12       31.03        32.2         386.7           23%

  From 1 to 12 cores the AGGREGATE throughput grows 2.8x, not 12x.
  Each core, alone, did 140.2 M accesses/s; with 12 active it does
  32.2 M -- 23% of what it did alone. Bandwidth does not multiply
  per core: it is divided.

  And the design consequence: sizing a data plane from the
  measurement of ONE lcore overestimates the whole system. The number
  that matters is the bottom row of this table, not the top one.
