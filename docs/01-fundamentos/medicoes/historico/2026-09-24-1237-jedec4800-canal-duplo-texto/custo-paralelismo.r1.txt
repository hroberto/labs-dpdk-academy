   K   ns/access   M accesses/s   batch of K ready in   throughput gain
  ---  ---------   -----------   ---------------------   --------------
    1      88.47        11.3                88 ns            1.0x
    2      43.33        23.1                87 ns            2.0x
    4      23.32        42.9                93 ns            3.8x
    8      12.22        81.8                98 ns            7.2x
   12       8.32       120.3               100 ns           10.6x
   16       6.35       157.4               102 ns           13.9x
   32       3.55       281.3               114 ns           24.9x
   64       2.75       363.7               176 ns           32.2x

  The latency of ONE access is ~88 ns and does not change in any row.
  What changes is how many of them happen at the same time.

  With K = 1 this machine does 11.3 M accesses/s -- BELOW the 14.9 M
  packets/s of the 10 GbE line rate with 64 B frames. A single
  dependent access per packet already loses the rate, before any
  processing. With K = 64 there is 2344% of headroom left.

  And the price: the batch of 64 is only ready in 176 ns, against 88 ns
  for the lone access. Throughput bought with latency -- the budget of
  67.2 ns per packet says how much of that trade you can afford.

  --------   ---------   -----------   -----------   ------------
         1        6.56       152.4         152.4          100%

         8       11.84        84.5         675.9           55%
        12       17.19        58.2         698.0           38%

  From 1 to 12 cores the AGGREGATE throughput grows 4.6x, not 12x.
  Each core, alone, did 152.4 M accesses/s; with 12 active it does
  58.2 M -- 38% of what it did alone. Bandwidth does not multiply
  per core: it is divided.

  And the design consequence: sizing a data plane from the
  measurement of ONE lcore overestimates the whole system. The number
  that matters is the bottom row of this table, not the top one.
