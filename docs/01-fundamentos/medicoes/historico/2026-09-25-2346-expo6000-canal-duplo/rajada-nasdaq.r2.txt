  4. THE DIVISOR IS THE NUMBER OF CHANNELS, NOT OF QUEUES (ring fixed at 1024)

    chan  queue  ideal.div   burst rho      loss  p99(us)   note
    ----  -----  ---------   ---------      ----  -------  ----------------------------------------
       1      1          1        2.84   21.648%    750.6  TotalView-ITCH 4.1, one queue
       1      4          1        2.84   21.648%    750.6  the SAME feed with 4 RSS queues
       1     16          1        2.84   21.648%    750.6  and with 16 queues
       3      3          3        0.95    0.000%     25.0  the SAME traffic partitioned into 3 channels (ideal)
       8      8          8        0.36    0.000%      2.1  the SAME traffic partitioned into 8 channels (ideal)

     The first three rows are IDENTICAL: 16 queues do not help a
     single-channel feed. `ideal.div` is the CEILING of what
     partitioning can yield; the effective divisor depends on the RSS
     hash and has to be measured on the card.

  5. SCENARIO B -- the published guidance: burst of up to 2000 MESSAGES
     on a millisecond scale (TotalView-ITCH FPGA FAQ)

     The FAQ speaks of MESSAGES; the ring counts DESCRIPTORS. THREE
     things here are PREMISE, not source: the 1 ms window (the FAQ
     says only "millisecond scale"), the 40 B per ITCH message (size
     varies with type) and the packing, which is the axis of the
     table precisely because it changes the result the most.

   msgs/datagram   load(B)    wire(B)   datagrams     implied Gb/s   drained/ms     excess
   -------------   -------    -------   ---------     ------------   ----------     ------
               1        40        106        2000             1.70         1366        634
               2        80        146        1000             1.17         1366          0
               4       160        226         500             0.90         1366          0
               8       320        386         250             0.77         1366          0
              16       640        706         125             0.71         1366          0
              32      1280       1346          63             0.68         1366          0
              64      2560       2626          32             0.67         1366          0   <- exceeds 1500 B under any reading

     Three different rates on the same row: MESSAGES (the source),
     DATAGRAMS (what the ring counts) and BITS (what the link carries).
     Confusing them is the classic RX sizing mistake.

     The two scenarios, in the SAME quantity -- queue excess:
       A (link limit, mean burst of 1 ms)      : 2516 descriptors
       B (2000 msgs in 1 ms, 1 msg/datagram)   : 634 descriptors
       B with 2 messages per datagram          : 0

     There is no contradiction between the sources: they answer
     different questions. A asks "what if the link is filled to the
     limit?"; B, "what follows from the published guidance?".
     A is deliberately conservative; B is anchored in the number of
     messages the exchange publishes, but STILL requires premises to
     convert messages into datagrams and duration into rate.
