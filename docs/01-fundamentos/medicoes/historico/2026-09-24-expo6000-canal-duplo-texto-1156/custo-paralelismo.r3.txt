   K   ns/access   M accesses/s   batch of K ready in   throughput gain
  ---  ---------   -----------   ---------------------   --------------
    1      77.34        12.9                77 ns            1.0x

    4      20.55        48.7                82 ns            3.8x

   12       7.38       135.5                89 ns           10.5x
   16       5.63       177.5                90 ns           13.7x
   32       3.20       312.7               102 ns           24.2x
   64       2.44       409.6               156 ns           31.7x

  The latency of ONE access is ~77 ns and does not change in any row.
  What changes is how many of them happen at the same time.

  With K = 1 this machine does 12.9 M accesses/s -- BELOW the 14.9 M
  packets/s of the 10 GbE line rate with 64 B frames. A single
  dependent access per packet already loses the rate, before any
  processing. With K = 64 there is 2652% of headroom left.

  And the price: the batch of 64 is only ready in 156 ns, against 77 ns
  for the lone access. Throughput bought with latency -- the budget of
  67.2 ns per packet says how much of that trade you can afford.

        12       12.66        79.0         947.6           46%

  From 1 to 12 cores the AGGREGATE throughput grows 5.5x, not 12x.
  Each core, alone, did 170.9 M accesses/s; with 12 active it does
  79.0 M -- 46% of what it did alone. Bandwidth does not multiply
  per core: it is divided.

  And the design consequence: sizing a data plane from the
  measurement of ONE lcore overestimates the whole system. The number
  that matters is the bottom row of this table, not the top one.
