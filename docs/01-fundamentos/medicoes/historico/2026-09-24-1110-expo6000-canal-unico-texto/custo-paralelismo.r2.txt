  What the table above means, column by column:

   K   ns/access   M accesses/s   batch of K ready in   throughput gain
  ---  ---------   -----------   ---------------------   --------------
    1      78.69        12.7                79 ns            1.0x
    2      38.87        25.7                78 ns            2.0x
    4      21.14        47.3                85 ns            3.7x
    8      11.19        89.4                90 ns            7.0x
   12       7.77       128.7                93 ns           10.1x
   16       6.03       165.9                96 ns           13.1x
   32       3.61       277.3               115 ns           21.8x
   64       2.86       349.6               183 ns           27.5x

  The latency of ONE access is ~79 ns and does not change in any row.
  What changes is how many of them happen at the same time.

  With K = 1 this machine does 12.7 M accesses/s -- BELOW the 14.9 M
  packets/s of the 10 GbE line rate with 64 B frames. A single
  dependent access per packet already loses the rate, before any
  processing. With K = 64 there is 2249% of headroom left.

  And the price: the batch of 64 is only ready in 183 ns, against 79 ns
  for the lone access. Throughput bought with latency -- the budget of
  67.2 ns per packet says how much of that trade you can afford.

  --------   ---------   -----------   -----------   ------------
         1        6.36       157.1         157.1          100%

         8       14.91        67.1         536.5           43%
        12       22.14        45.2         542.1           29%

  From 1 to 12 cores the AGGREGATE throughput grows 3.4x, not 12x.
  Each core, alone, did 157.1 M accesses/s; with 12 active it does
  45.2 M -- 29% of what it did alone. Bandwidth does not multiply
  per core: it is divided.

  And the design consequence: sizing a data plane from the
  measurement of ONE lcore overestimates the whole system. The number
  that matters is the bottom row of this table, not the top one.
